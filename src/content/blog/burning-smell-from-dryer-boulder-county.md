A burning smell from the dryer means stop the load. Lint may be on the heater or in the duct, the air may have nowhere to go, or something from a pocket may have melted. A heating element or a thermostat can fail too. A vent cleaning fixes the duct. It does not fix the machine. The drawing above is that path.

## Stop the dryer first

Turn the cycle off. Unplug an electric dryer, or shut off its breaker. On a gas dryer, turn the machine off and leave the gas connector alone. Do not start another load to see if the smell comes back.

If you see smoke, or any flame, leave the house and call 911. Do not open the dryer to look.

If there is no smoke and no flame, let the machine cool before you look. Do not run the dryer until you know where the smell came from.

## Common causes

**Lint in the vent.** The screen catches the mat you can peel off. Finer lint still moves into the duct and settles where the air slows. A restricted duct holds heat in the dryer, and lint near that heat can scorch. The smell is often stronger in the laundry room than in the clothes. [The lint screen and the vent are different jobs](/blog/lint-screen-vs-dryer-vent-cleaning-boulder-county/).

**Lint around the drum or on the heater.** Lint that misses the screen can fall behind the drum. On an electric dryer it can land on the heating element and char. On a gas dryer it can sit near the burner housing. The smell may fade, then come back if more lint is still in the cabinet. Cleaning the vent does not reach the element. That lint is inside the dryer.

**A crushed transition duct.** The short connector from the dryer to the pipe in the wall flattens when the machine is pushed in. A kink, a sag, or a foil hose mashed flat is a pinch, so the dryer runs hot. [The duct behind the dryer](/blog/foil-vs-metal-dryer-vent-duct-boulder-county/) is the piece you can see. On a gas dryer, do not pull, kink, or stretch the gas connector. If you smell gas, stop and leave it alone.

**A hood flap that is stuck.** Lint in the mouth, a flap that will not swing, or louvers packed shut keep the exhaust in the pipe. [How to check the outdoor hood](/blog/how-to-check-outdoor-dryer-vent-hood/) covers that look. Stay off the ladder and off the roof.

**Something from a pocket.** A crayon, lip balm, a tissue, or a bit of plastic can melt on the drum. The smell is often sweet or waxy, and you may see a mark once the drum is cool. Empty the other pockets. If a dry cloth will not lift the mark, call an appliance tech. That is not a duct problem.

**A new dryer.** Oils on a new heating element can smell like hot metal for the first few cycles. Stay nearby, with a window open. The smell should fade. Smoke, a smell that gets stronger, or a smell that remains after those cycles is not ordinary burn-off. Stop and have it checked.

## What you can check yourself

**The lint screen.** Pull it, clear the mat, and put it back. If dryer sheets have left a film, wash the screen in warm water and let it dry fully. A full screen is its own restriction.

**The transition duct.** After the dryer is cool and unplugged, slide it out just far enough to see the connector. It should be round, short, and not coiled on the floor. A flat spot is a pinch. Do not disconnect a gas line to get a better view.

**The outside hood.** On a hood you can reach from the yard, look at the flap. Lint in the mouth, or a flap that stays shut, is a blockage you can see. Only if there was no smoke, a short heated cycle shows the airflow: the flap should open, and you should feel warm air a few inches out. If the smell returns as soon as the heat comes on, stop and unplug the dryer.

## Cold months on the Front Range

In winter, more laundry stays indoors. Heavier loads mean longer cycles, and dry Front Range air makes fine lint cling inside the duct. Snow or ice against a low hood can hold the flap shut the same way lint does. Clear snow from a hood you can reach from the ground. Do not chip ice off the flap. If the dryer smells like something is burning, stop it. The season does not change that.

## Vent cleaning, or an appliance repair

[Dryer vent cleaning](/dryer-vent-cleaning/) clears lint from the connector, the duct, and the hood. Book it when the screen is clean and the air at the hood is weak, when lint is sitting in the hood, when the dryer top is hot, or when it has been about a year. Those signs are in [five signs your duct is packed](/blog/five-signs-your-duct-is-packed/).

A vent cleaning does not repair the dryer. It does not replace a heating element or a thermostat. If the duct is open, the flap moves, and the smell is still there, call an appliance repair tech. The same is true for a melted object on the drum, and for a new dryer that still smells when the vent is already clear.

A crushed connector is a shape problem. Brushing it does not make a flat hose round. A new duct, cap, or dryer part is not in the flat rate.
